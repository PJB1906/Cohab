# Cohab v2 — Human study protocol and pre-registration

Status: **fixed before data collection.** Any change after the first real
rater is recorded in §9 with its date and reason. Timestamp this file (for
example on OSF) before recruitment opens.

Two studies, both anonymous and both outside the product (no Cohab account):

| Study | Question | Who | Time | Target n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cohab Lab (W2) | Which lifestyle attributes do people actually weigh when choosing a roommate? | Anyone | ~20 min | 50 raters (≈1,600 comparisons) |
| Roommate pairs (W3) | Does the Cohab score track how real cohabitation went? | Two people who live or lived together | ~10 min each | 40 pairs |

## 1. Ethics and consent

- Consent versions: `lab-2026-11-v1`, `pairs-2026-11-v1` (`services/study/instrument.py`).
  The consent text is shown in full before any answer is stored; the API rejects
  any other version string.
- Collected: lifestyle answers, choices, confidence, optional 140-character
  reasons, response times, optional coarse demographics. Never collected: name,
  email, phone, institution ID, IP address in study tables, or any product data.
- Tokens are random; only their SHA-256 is stored. A participant can withdraw at
  the end of the Lab, which deletes every row they produced.
- Profiles shown in the Lab are generated for the study. None is a real person.
- Department-level approval to be confirmed with the guide before recruitment.

## 2. Instrument

**Rater's own answers.** The 21 lifestyle fields the product asks (18 required
plus the four tier-2 items), so every attribute's directional score from the
rater is defined, plus up to three "most important" attributes.

**Cards.** Each card shows only the card person's behaviour (16 fields:
`CARD_FIELDS`). Tolerances are not shown, so the choice measures one direction,
S(rater → card), which is exactly what the features encode. Budget, interests
and free text are held out.

## 3. Design (Lab)

Per rater, 40 trials, generated by `services/study/design.py` from a stored seed:

| Kind | n | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pair | 32 | Two cards identical except on 5 fields; fields balanced so each varies about equally often. Left/right randomised. |
| Repeat | 4 | An earlier pair shown again with sides swapped, at least 6 trials later. Test–retest reliability and the noise ceiling. |
| Attention | 3 | Best possible card for this rater against the worst. Directional score gap > 0.4 by construction. |
| Rank | 1 | Six independent cards to order best to worst. Kendall τ. |

Each pair trial records: choice (A, B, no preference), confidence 1–5, would
message each card (either, both, neither), optional reason, response time.

**Why designed pairs** (deviation from the v1 deck): pairs sampled from the
seeded pool confound correlated attributes, and cards that differ on every row
push raters to attend to three or four of them. See D-14.5.

## 4. Primary hypothesis and analysis (pre-registered)

**H1.** Weights learned from choices predict held-out raters' choices better
than the expert weight set v2.

- **Model.** Bradley–Terry over feature differences:
  P(A ≻ B) = σ(Σ_k β_k · m_Rk · [s_k(R→A) − s_k(R→B)]), β ≥ 0,
  m_Rk = 1 + γ if R marked k important. L2 shrinkage toward the expert prior
  τ₀·w_expert, λ chosen by grouped 5-fold CV on the training raters.
- **Evaluation.** Grouped 5-fold CV by rater (a rater is never in train and test).
- **Primary metric.** Learned − expert log loss on held-out raters, with a 95%
  interval from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of raters.
- **Decision rule.** Interval entirely below 0: learned is better. Entirely above
  0: worse. Spanning 0: no reliable difference. All three are reported as
  findings; a null is not a failure.
- **Reported beside it.** Accuracy and log loss for random, best single
  attribute, uniform, expert and learned; noise ceiling from repeats; Kendall τ on
  the rank task; estimated "top 3" multiplier against v1's 1.5 with interval;
  learning curve.

**Sample size.** Justified by `analysis/power.py` (results in
`docs/results/power.json`): the number of raters at which the pipeline detects a
plausible departure from the expert weights with 80% probability, across seven
scenarios of how real raters deviate from the model.

## 5. Exclusions (fixed now, applied before any fit)

A rater is excluded if any holds (`analysis/reliability.py`):

1. Failed two or more of the three attention checks.
2. Median response time on pair trials below 1,500 ms.
3. Fewer than 20 answered pair trials.
4. "No preference" on more than half of pair trials.

"No preference" trials are dropped from the fit (count reported). Repeat,
attention and rank trials are not used to fit weights.

## 6. Roommate pairs (W3)

Each person, alone: the 21 lifestyle answers; how their roommate would describe
them on five gameable items; eight outcome items (1–5); months together; whether
still together.

Pre-registered analyses (`analysis/pair_analysis.py`), each with 2,000 bootstrap
resamples of pairs:

1. **Known groups.** Mean Cohab score of satisfied pairs (mean overall
   satisfaction ≥ 4) minus dissatisfied pairs; Spearman ρ between score and mean
   satisfaction.
2. **Permutation null.** Mean score of real pairs against 5,000 random
   re-pairings of the same people; one-sided p.
3. **Self-report inflation.** Per gameable item, agreement between a person's
   own answer and their roommate's description of them, and (for ordinal items)
   the mean number of steps the self-description is more flattering.

Stated limits: survivorship (pairs who split early are under-sampled), answers
coloured by having lived together, small n, no multiplicity correction.

## 7. Calibration and stability (W4)

- Out-of-fold isotonic map from S(rater → card) to P(would message). Metrics:
  ECE (target < 0.05), Brier, log loss, slope and intercept, reliability bins.
  Compared against using the raw score as a probability.
- Rank stability: top-5 Jaccard and P(#1 changes) on a 1,000-agent population
  under the rater-bootstrap weight vectors.

## 8. Gate 2 (end of week 6) and tiers

| Tier | Rule | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | ≥ 40 kept raters and ≥ 30 complete pairs | Nothing. |
| B | ≥ 20 kept raters | Fit 6 attribute clusters (expert proportions within each), multiplier fixed at 1.5. |
| C | ≥ 8 kept raters | Report as a pilot: reliability, completion, instrument findings. |
| D | fewer | Simulation, power, sensitivity and the public-data transfer test carry the report. |

Collection stops at the end of week 7 whatever the count. Real participants are
never supplemented with synthetic or LLM-generated responses.
